# English writing profile

Use this profile for English copy and prose. The canonical rules are language-agnostic; this profile adds English register and formatting cautions. It does not require a fixed dialect or tone — a changelog, essay, forum answer, and campaign page have different legitimate voices.

## Review order

1. **Answer and purpose.** Can a reader identify the main answer, decision, or action near the start? Is the background needed to understand it?
2. **Claims and evidence.** Keep attribution, source scope, uncertainty, units, dates, conditions, subjects, and comparisons exact.
3. **Information density.** Find repeated meaning, throat-clearing, abstract phrases hiding an actor or action, and headings that promise no content.
4. **Voice and rhythm.** Check uniform sentence length, repeated contrast patterns (“not just X, but Y”), mechanically parallel triads, promotional modifiers, and register elevation in context.
5. **Structure.** Keep headings and bullets when they help scanning. Remove formatting the medium does not need.

## English-specific cautions

- Do not flag a single mark or word. Measured catalogs treat the em dash, typographic quotes, and short-sentence cadence as weak or negative signals; density and repetition are the evidence (S16).
- The S16 catalog classifications recorded in the evidence registry label uniform cadence and hedge-adverb density (“usually”, “mostly”, “typically”, “generally”, “slightly”) active, the older hedging cluster (“might”, “could”, “perhaps”) fading, and words such as “delve” retired. These are the catalog author's classifications with its stated methodology limits, not independently established detector performance. Review repetition and lost information in the actual passage; preserve a hedge when it expresses real uncertainty (W14/W28). Do not turn a changing catalog into a word blacklist.
- Assistant-voice residue: “Great question!”, “Certainly!”, “Hope this helps!”, “Let me know if you have any other questions.” In deliverables these are removable scaffolding (W16).
- Scene-setting openers (“In today's rapidly evolving world”) that postpone the answer are a review cue (W15); significance asserted without support is W05.
- Register elevation — “utilize”, “leverage”, “culinary”, “bespoke”, “journey” — replacing the plain term the audience uses (“use”, “cooking”, “custom”, “trip”) is a warning, not an error (W29).
- Keep quotes, code, units, URLs, named entities, and meaningful sequence exact.

## Minimal revision

Use a supplied sample or applicable project guide to retain dialect, contractions, purposeful asides, fragments, and ambivalence. Do not force informality, add an opinion, or strip an intentional rhetorical habit just because a generic profile prefers another register.

Review whole paragraphs too: heading restatements, repeated lesson-like closers, and invented objections can survive a vocabulary pass (W03, W13). In a reply, omit shared background only when the prior context is available (W15); a standalone explanation may need it. For drafting and long-document consistency, use [editorial-method.md](editorial-method.md).

Prefer a local change: remove a repeated clause, name the actor, state the action, make attribution specific, or move the direct answer ahead of context. Preserve the claim's strength — changing “might work” to “is proven” is a factual change, not a style edit. Never replace a weakly supported claim with a made-up number, feature, citation, or first-person anecdote.
