# Korean writing profile

Use this profile for Korean copy and prose. It favors concrete information and natural rhythm; it does not require short sentences, plain vocabulary, or one fixed register. A technical report, essay, campaign line, interview, poem, and legal notice have different legitimate voices.

## Review order

1. **Answer and purpose.** Can a reader identify the main answer, decision, or action near the start? Is the background needed to understand it?
2. **Claims and evidence.** Keep attribution, source scope, uncertainty, units, dates, conditions, subjects, and comparisons exact. Remove an unsupported assertion only when it is not part of the user's intended claim; otherwise flag it.
3. **Information density.** Find repeated meaning, generic transitions, abstract nouns hiding an actor or action, and headings that promise no content.
4. **Voice and rhythm.** Check repeated contrast patterns, mechanically parallel lists, clipped sentence runs, promotional modifiers, and punctuation amplification in context.
5. **Structure.** Keep headings and bullets when they help scanning. Remove only redundant labels, empty sections, or a table whose columns do not compare useful dimensions.

## Korean-specific cautions

- Korean abstract nouns such as 혁신, 본질, 가치, 차원, 패러다임, 핵심, 시너지 are not prohibited. Check internally what changed, who acts, what evidence supports it, or what the noun refers to. Warn only when an abstract or grand word adds no checkable content.
- Watch nominal chains, forced coined compounds, excessive parentheses, repeated ellipses or exclamation marks, and repeated sentence-final patterns. Keep terms of art, established product names, literary effects, and parentheses that carry necessary conditions.
- A repeated “A가 아니다, B다” construction, neat three-part list, bold label followed by a colon, or colon in a title is a signal to inspect the surrounding passage, never an automatic error.
- A genuine three-item list, one purposeful contrast, a title/subtitle pair that conveys different information, or a short sentence run used for effect can be the clearest choice.
- Do not infer a source from “연구에 따르면”, “전문가들은”, “알려져 있다”, or “많은 사람이”. Preserve or flag an unresolved attribution; follow the execution contract before asking for missing evidence. Do not fabricate a source.
- Assistant-voice residue in Korean shows as sycophantic openers (“물론입니다!”, “좋은 질문이네요!”), formulaic closers (“도움이 되셨길 바랍니다”), and process narration; in deliverables these are removable scaffolding (W16).
- Watch interpretation tails (“이는 ~을 보여 줍니다”, “~을 강조합니다”) and qualifier piles (“~일 수 있습니다” on every claim, one disclaimer repeated). Cut what the text does not support; keep required disclosures once where they apply (W27, W28).
- Watch calqued imagery (“풍부한 태피스트리”, “인생의 여정”, “깊이 파헤쳐 봅시다”) and register-elevated words replacing the plain term the audience uses (W29).
- A uniform rhythm — near-equal sentence lengths, one ending, 균질한 격식 — reads as monotone even when each sentence is fine (W14). Connective openers (또한, 따라서, 즉, 다만) in every paragraph are a review cue, not an error by themselves.
- Match structure to the medium: a chat reply or short post with headers, bullet piles, bold bursts, and emoji structure carries formatting the medium does not need (W11).

## Minimal revision

Use a supplied sample or applicable project guide to preserve 종결어미, 존댓말 수준, intentional fragments, asides, and unresolved feelings. Do not change “했어” to “했습니다” merely to polish it, or add slang to simulate a person. If the user asks for a different register, apply that request while keeping factual claims intact.

Review whole paragraphs too: a heading restated in its first sentence, the same interpretive closer after each example, or an unneeded objection can add structure without information (W03, W13). In a reply, use visible prior context to decide whether background is redundant (W15); a standalone 안내문 may still need it. For drafting or long-document consistency, use [editorial-method.md](editorial-method.md).

Prefer a local change: remove a repeated clause, name the actor, state the action, make attribution specific, or move the direct answer ahead of context. Preserve the claim's strength. For example, changing “가능성이 있다” to “입증됐다” is a factual change, not a style edit. Never replace a weakly supported claim with a made-up percentage, feature, citation, or first-person anecdote.
