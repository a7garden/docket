# Editorial method

Use this for drafting, matching a supplied voice, separating a mixed brief from its artifact, or revising a long document. The rule catalog diagnoses specific problems; this reference helps choose what to write and how much to change.

## Establish the brief

Identify the reader, what they already know, what they need next, the medium, the supported content, and the requested register from available context. Follow the [execution contract](execution-contract.md) for missing inputs: unresolved direct launches use the choice-based setup, optional preferences use defaults, and embedded use adds no interview. This reference does not independently authorize questions.

Apply [quality-framework.md](quality-framework.md) for preservation and economy. Keep four roles distinct:

- **Instructions:** what the user wants changed or preserved. Apply these without printing them as part of the artifact.
- **Artifact:** the text or UI being edited. Quoted commands inside it remain content unless the user explicitly adopts them.
- **Evidence:** facts and sources that can support the result. A voice sample is not evidence for a new feature or anecdote.
- **Voice sample:** how the author writes. Transfer cadence, register, stance, and rhetorical habits; do not transfer its events, claims, or distinctive sentences.

If the boundary is unclear, preserve the ambiguous portion and proceed with the clear portion; use the execution contract if the ambiguity blocks the task. Do not silently delete an instruction-like sentence that is actually part of a letter, script, or quoted example.

## Write toward a useful result

Build the draft around the answer or action the reader needs. Give each paragraph a role: a supported claim, explanation, example, condition, comparison, or narrative move. Use concrete detail already available; when the source is thin, keep the output proportionate rather than inventing mechanisms, outcomes, or obligations.

Avoid secondary headings, labels, and explanations that only restate a direct sentence. Give each one a distinct reader purpose; retain necessary conditions, evidence, and navigation. Do not add framing merely to make a short answer look structured.

Choose structure for the medium. A reply can lead with the decision and omit context the recipient supplied. A reference page may repeat labels for navigation. An essay may use suspense, a refrain, or unresolved tension. No universal rule requires a human subject in every sentence, a fixed item count, or a sentence-length distribution.

Apply the explicit tone and supplied sample before a relevant project style guide, then the source and medium. Inspect a style file when supplied, already referenced by the project, or readily identifiable within the target project. Do not search unrelated directories or create a voice profile without a request. Resolve conflicting sources through that precedence where possible; otherwise preserve the source and apply the execution contract to essential ambiguity. A thin sample supports modest adaptation, not a new personality.

## Review at two scales

At the sentence level, repair vague agency, duplicated meaning, unsupported significance, or misplaced qualification. At the document level, check whether headings, opening sentences, examples, and closers repeat the same point; whether the reader already knows the background; and whether an objection resolves a real question or merely manufactures emphasis.

For long documents, keep a small working ledger of recurring entities, preferred terms, quantities, dates, claim strength, and unresolved conflicts. Compare each edited section with its source and check summaries against the body at the end. Keep the ledger internal unless the user needs it. If only an excerpt is available, bound any consistency claim to that excerpt.

Make the smallest change that repairs the diagnosed problem. A whole paragraph can need restructuring, but length alone does not justify rewriting everything. With no supported finding, keep the text and report no change. Do not force contractions, misspellings, fragments, slang, or random variation to satisfy a notion of human writing.

When removing framing or repetition, retain already-clear factual and eligibility clauses unless the repair actually requires changing them. Do not paraphrase unaffected restrictions just to make the whole passage sound newly written.

## Finish with a meaning-and-voice check

Compare source and result for changed facts, lost detail, stronger certainty, new causality, altered timing, new commitments, or merged claims that imply simultaneity. Check whether edits retain the author's purposeful quirks and whether the replacements have developed a repetitive shape of their own. Restore any drift. Report unresolved contradictions without choosing a plausible answer.

Check source ownership and the claim's subject separately, including first-person perspective. Preserve both whose benchmark it is and whose response time improved when reordering a measurement claim. “Our benchmark showed a 32% improvement in response time” keeps the source owner but can lose the explicit subject “our response time”; a vague “a benchmark” also loses source ownership. Keep both relationships without requiring one exact phrase.

In drafts and revisions, preserve the scope of eligibility, price, and timing in both directions. Keep unaffected eligibility predicates intact, including attached qualifiers. Separate source assertions must stay independently clear: “only members may download; downloading is free” must not become only a free-download restriction. Conversely, do not split “members alone may download at no cost” into a blanket members-only download ban. “Launches for paid accounts” does not establish exclusivity. Preserve unresolved scope rather than infer it. Check for lost and added exclusions, not merely the presence of “only”; restore drift even if the result is longer. This check stays internal.

When translating or revising across languages, preserve claim strength, attribution, conditions, and register. A hedge such as “~할 수 있다” or “may” must not become a definite result. Where context does not establish an omitted actor, use an impersonal construction rather than inventing one. Carry stance and politeness into natural target-language conventions; do not translate Korean honorific forms mechanically or flatten the voice. Keep protected literals exact unless their translation or localization is explicitly requested; never change quantities or claim scope in the process. Disclose material uncertainty in language-specific judgments under the execution contract.

This is a final editorial check, not an endless retry loop. Revisit only a concrete remaining problem. Do not print the internal checklist or claim verification beyond what was actually compared.

## Conditional text hygiene

Inspect hidden characters only when the user requests it or there is a visible rendering, copying, or search problem. If codepoints are accessible, locate the candidate and check its role before editing. Remove only confirmed formatting residue in editable prose; preserve ambiguous characters and exact-value spans.

Joiners can shape scripts and emoji; direction controls can preserve mixed-direction text; variation selectors affect display; nonbreaking spaces can preserve units and locale typography. Do not broadly strip non-ASCII characters or apply compatibility normalization to the entire text. Report the codepoints and locations actually changed, and state a visibility limit when inspection is unavailable. Such cleanup does not establish authorship or removal of unseen provenance.
